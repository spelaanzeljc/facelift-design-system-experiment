# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Facelift serves a wide range of clients and users with meaningfully different profiles:

- **Social media managers at brands** — single-brand operators, primarily scheduling and publishing content across channels, monitoring engagement, and executing campaigns on tight deadlines.
- **Agencies managing multiple clients** — multi-account operators who context-switch constantly, need strong structural separation between clients, and must coordinate approvals, handoffs, and reporting across many accounts simultaneously.
- **Enterprise marketing teams** — larger teams with distinct roles (editors, approvers, analysts, community managers) who depend on structured workflows, audit trails, and permissions to operate at scale.

Across all profiles, the primary job is managing the full social content lifecycle: plan, create, approve, schedule, publish, monitor, respond, report.

## Product Purpose

Facelift is a professional social media management platform. It exists to give marketing teams — regardless of size or structure — confident control over their entire social presence: content planning (calendar/orchestrate), campaign execution, community engagement (inbox/engage), and AI-assisted workflows.

Success looks like: users finishing their day knowing nothing slipped through the cracks. The tool should be a reliable operations layer, not a distraction.

## Brand Personality

Professional. Efficient. Reliable.

Facelift earns trust by being fast, consistent, and never surprising. The interface recedes so the work can advance. Think Linear's focus and directness, applied to marketing operations.

Tone is calm and expert — never playful for playfulness's sake, never terse to the point of being cold. It respects that users are skilled professionals operating under real pressure.

## Anti-references

- **Loud agency tools**: heavy gradients, flashy micro-animations, visual design that performs excitement instead of delivering usefulness. Facelift should not look like it's trying to impress anyone — it should look like it works.
- **Hootsuite / Buffer aesthetic**: cluttered, legacy feel with inconsistent hierarchy and cramped tables. That era of social tools.

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity under load** — marketing workflows involve high information density: calendars, feed items, data tables, multi-account filters. Every layout decision should reduce cognitive load, not just look clean when empty.

2. **Structure earns trust** — the design's job is to communicate that things are in order. Consistent spacing, predictable patterns, stable navigation. Surprises break confidence.

3. **The tool disappears** — a great interface doesn't make users think about the interface. Task completion is the measure, not delight at the UI.

4. **Respect the professional** — no hand-holding copy, no patronizing empty states, no onboarding that talks down to an expert. Users know what they're doing; the tool should match their fluency.

5. **Modularity without fragmentation** — Facelift has many modules (campaigns, calendar, inbox, orchestrate, feed, AI). Visual cohesion across modules is non-negotiable. Same tokens, same patterns, same spatial logic everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal compliance requirement, but target WCAG 2.1 AA as closely as possible in practice. Priority areas: text contrast ratios (especially in the dark header and dense table views), keyboard navigation for common workflows, and sufficient focus indicators. Reduced-motion support for animations is expected.
